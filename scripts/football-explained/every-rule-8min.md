# Every Football Rule Explained In 8 Minutes

**Channel style:** football explained (beginner-focused, modular rule-per-chapter)
**Target runtime:** ~8 minutes (~1,250 words at ~150 wpm)
**Format key:** `[CARD]` = segment title card · `[NARRATOR]` = animated presenter at whiteboard · `[DIAGRAM]` = animated pitch/graphic · `[FOOTAGE]` = real match clips

---

## OPEN — 0:00
> [CARD: whistle icon] **Everything.**
> [NARRATOR] Football looks simple — kick the ball into the net. But there are dozens of rules running underneath every match, and most fans only half-understand them.
> [DIAGRAM: rulebook flipping open, rules flying out]
> So here's every rule that actually matters, in 8 minutes. By the end, you'll never be confused watching a game again. Let's start with the point of the whole thing.

## 1 — The Objective — 0:18
> [CARD: "The Aim"]
> [NARRATOR] Two teams of 11. Score more goals than the other team before time runs out.
> [DIAGRAM: top-down pitch, red vs blue dots, scoreboard 0–1]
> A goal counts only when the *whole* ball crosses the *whole* line between the posts. Most goals, draws if level — and in knockouts, extra time or penalties decide it. That's the entire game. Everything else is just *how*.

## 2 — No Hands — 0:40
> [CARD: "Handball"]
> [NARRATOR] The biggest rule: you can use any part of your body *except* your hands and arms.
> [DIAGRAM: body figure — head, chest, knee, foot all green; arms red]
> A handball is called when you deliberately touch the ball, or your arm is in an unnatural position that makes your body bigger.
> [FOOTAGE: defender running with arms tucked behind back]
> That's why you'll see defenders run with arms pinned behind their backs. One exception to the no-hands rule — *more on him later.*

## 3 — Kick-Off — 1:00
> [CARD: "Kick-Off"]
> [NARRATOR] Every match — and every restart after a goal — begins with a kick-off from the center spot.
> [DIAGRAM: ball on center spot, one team inside the circle, other team kept out]
> A coin toss decides who starts. The other team must stay outside the center circle until the ball's in play. You'll also see kick-offs at the start of each half.

## 4 — Throw-Ins — 1:20
> [CARD: "Throw-Ins"]
> [NARRATOR] When the ball crosses the *sideline*, it comes back with a throw-in.
> [DIAGRAM: both feet behind the line, ball thrown over the head with two hands]
> Both feet stay on the ground, and you throw from behind and over your head — or it's handed to the other team.
> [FOOTAGE: long throw launched into the box]
> You can't score directly from one. It's just a way to restart the attack.

## 5 — Corners & Goal Kicks — 1:42
> [CARD: "Corners & Goal Kicks"]
> [NARRATOR] When the ball crosses the *goal line* but doesn't go in, one of two things happens.
> [DIAGRAM: ball off an attacker → goal kick; ball off a defender → corner]
> If a defender touched it last, the attacking team gets a corner — kicked from the corner arc, a huge scoring chance.
> [FOOTAGE: corner swung in, header, goal]
> If an attacker touched it last, it's a goal kick for the defending team, taken from inside the six-yard box.

## 6 — Fouls — 2:10
> [CARD: "Fouls"]
> [NARRATOR] Football is physical, but not a free-for-all.
> [DIAGRAM: bullet list — "Unfair" and "Dangerous"]
> Kicking, tripping, pushing, holding shirts, or sliding in and missing the ball — that's a foul.
> [FOOTAGE: montage of trips and pulls, ref's whistle]
> The other team gets a free kick. And if the foul happened inside the box — *more on that in a second.*

## 7 — Free Kicks — 2:35
> [CARD: "Free Kicks"]
> [NARRATOR] A free kick restarts play after a foul. There are two types.
> [DIAGRAM: "Direct" — arrow straight to goal; "Indirect" — must touch a second player first]
> A direct free kick can be shot straight at goal — which is why you'll see a defensive "wall" of players lined up to block it.
> [FOOTAGE: free kick curled over the wall]
> An indirect free kick must touch another player before a goal counts.

## 8 — Penalty Kicks — 3:00
> [CARD: "Penalties"]
> [NARRATOR] A foul or handball *inside the box* is the big one — a penalty.
> [DIAGRAM: ball on the spot, 12 yards out, only kicker and keeper allowed in]
> It's one player versus the keeper from twelve yards. The keeper must stay on the line until it's kicked.
> [FOOTAGE: tense penalty, crowd roaring]
> One of the highest-pressure moments in sport — and shootouts decide tied knockout games.

## 9 — The Goalkeeper — 3:30
> [CARD: "The Goalkeeper"]
> [NARRATOR] Here's that exception to the no-hands rule. The goalkeeper can use their hands — but *only* inside their own penalty box.
> [DIAGRAM: 18-yard box highlighted green, everything outside it red]
> Catch, punch, or parry inside the box. Step outside it, and they're just like everyone else — no hands.
> [FOOTAGE: keeper flying across goal to make a save]

## 10 — Yellow & Red Cards — 3:58
> [CARD: "Cards"]
> [NARRATOR] Refs don't just blow whistles — they show cards.
> [DIAGRAM: yellow card "warning," red card "sent off"]
> A yellow is a warning, for things like reckless tackles or arguing. Two yellows in a match equals a red.
> [FOOTAGE: ref shows two yellows turning into a red, player walks off]
> A straight red is for violent or dangerous play. Sent off, you can't be replaced — your team plays the rest with ten men.

## 11 — Offside — 4:35
> [CARD: "Offside"]
> [NARRATOR] Football's most misunderstood rule — but it's simple. You're offside if you're closer to the goal than both the ball *and* the second-to-last defender at the moment the ball is passed to you.
> [DIAGRAM: offside line shifting with the defense, onside vs offside players marked]
> It's about where you were when the pass was *played*, not where you end up.
> [FOOTAGE: defenders stepping up to catch an attacker offside]
> Think of it as a line you can't sneak behind. And you can never be offside from a throw-in, corner, or goal kick.

## 12 — The Advantage Rule — 5:20
> [CARD: "Advantage"]
> [NARRATOR] Not every foul stops play.
> [DIAGRAM: player fouled, but teammate keeps the ball and runs at goal]
> If a team is fouled but keeps control and has a good attack, the ref can wave play on — letting them keep their advantage instead of stopping for a free kick.
> [FOOTAGE: ref runs with both arms stretched forward, attack continues]
> Both arms stretched out is the signal.

## 13 — Substitutions — 5:50
> [CARD: "Substitutions"]
> [NARRATOR] Teams can swap tired or injured players — most leagues now allow up to five subs.
> [DIAGRAM: player off, fresh player on; the off player crossed out]
> Once you're subbed off, you can't come back on. Coaches use subs for fresh legs or to change tactics.
> [FOOTAGE: fourth official holds up the substitution board]

## 14 — Match Length & Added Time — 6:20
> [CARD: "Time"]
> [NARRATOR] A match is 90 minutes — two halves of 45.
> [DIAGRAM: clock running, then "+5 MINS" board]
> But the clock never stops, so the ref adds time at the end of each half to make up for stoppages like injuries and subs.
> [FOOTAGE: dramatic added-time winner]
> Still tied in a knockout? Two 15-minute halves of extra time — then penalties.

## 15 — VAR — 6:55
> [CARD: "VAR"]
> [NARRATOR] Finally, the newest rule: VAR — the Video Assistant Referee. Football's instant replay.
> [DIAGRAM: monitor wall, offside lines being drawn]
> It double-checks the big calls — goals, penalties, red cards, and offsides — using multiple camera angles.
> [FOOTAGE: ref jogs to pitch-side screen, reviews, makes a decision]
> The on-field ref still makes the final call. It's not perfect, and it's often controversial — but it's here to get the big moments right.

## CLOSE + CTA — 7:30
> [NARRATOR] And that's it — every rule that runs a football match, from the kick-off to VAR.
> [DIAGRAM: all 15 segment icons light up in a grid]
> Now you can watch any game and understand *exactly* what's happening and why.

> Next video, we break down every position on the pitch — what each player actually does. Subscribe so you don't miss it.

## Production notes
- **Pace:** keep each shot ~5–8 seconds; never let the narrator linger. The energy *is* the retention.
- **Every rule = definition → diagram → real footage.** Don't skip the footage beat — it's what makes abstract rules click.
- **Retention loops built in:** "more on him later" (handball→keeper) and "more on that in a second" (fouls→penalties) pay off within the video; the CTA chains to the next episode.
- **Word count (~1,250)** lands at ~8 minutes at this channel's delivery speed. To tighten, cut Ch.7 (Free Kicks) or merge Corners/Goal Kicks.

## Accuracy checklist (verify against current IFAB Laws of the Game before recording)
- Substitution count (currently up to 5 in most competitions)
- Added-time / stoppage-time mechanics
- Handball wording (deliberate vs. unnatural position)
- Offside phrasing ("second-to-last defender," judged at moment of the pass)
